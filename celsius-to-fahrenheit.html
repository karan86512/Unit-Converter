Celsius to Fahrenheit: Formula & Examples

Learn how to convert Celsius to Fahrenheit and Fahrenheit to Celsius using simple formulas, examples and a practical temperature conversion table.

📖 8 min read • Updated July 2026

Celsius and Fahrenheit are two of the most commonly used temperature scales. If you need to understand a temperature given in another scale, knowing how to convert between Celsius and Fahrenheit is useful for travel, weather, cooking, science and everyday life.

What Is Celsius?

Celsius, represented by °C, is a temperature scale widely used around the world. It is part of the metric system and is commonly used for weather forecasts, cooking, science and everyday temperature measurements.

On the Celsius scale, water freezes at 0°C under standard atmospheric pressure and boils at 100°C.

Important Reference Points

Water freezes: 0°C

Water boils: 100°C

What Is Fahrenheit?

Fahrenheit, represented by °F, is another temperature scale. It is commonly used for everyday temperature measurements in the United States.

On the Fahrenheit scale, water freezes at 32°F and boils at 212°F under standard atmospheric pressure.

Important Reference Points

Water freezes: 32°F

Water boils: 212°F

Celsius to Fahrenheit Formula

To convert a temperature from Celsius to Fahrenheit, multiply the Celsius temperature by 9/5 and then add 32.

Celsius → Fahrenheit °F = (°C × 9/5) + 32

You can also write the formula as:

°F = (°C × 1.8) + 32

Example: Convert 20°C to Fahrenheit

Let's convert 20°C step by step.

°F = (20 × 9/5) + 32
°F = 36 + 32
°F = 68°F
Answer

20°C = 68°F

Fahrenheit to Celsius Formula

To convert Fahrenheit to Celsius, subtract 32 from the Fahrenheit temperature and then multiply the result by 5/9.

Fahrenheit → Celsius °C = (°F − 32) × 5/9

Example: Convert 68°F to Celsius

Start with the Fahrenheit temperature of 68°F.

°C = (68 − 32) × 5/9
°C = 36 × 5/9
°C = 20°C
Answer

68°F = 20°C

How to Convert Negative Celsius Temperatures

The same Celsius to Fahrenheit formula can be used for negative temperatures.

For example, let's convert -10°C.

°F = (-10 × 9/5) + 32
°F = -18 + 32
°F = 14°F
Answer

-10°C = 14°F

Common Celsius and Fahrenheit Conversion Mistakes

Temperature conversion is straightforward, but a few mistakes are common.

  • Forgetting to add 32 when converting Celsius to Fahrenheit.
  • Subtracting 32 at the wrong point in the Fahrenheit to Celsius formula.
  • Using 9/5 incorrectly as 5/9.
  • Rounding too early during a calculation.
  • Confusing Celsius and Fahrenheit symbols.

An Interesting Temperature: -40°

There is one particularly interesting point where Celsius and Fahrenheit have exactly the same numerical value.

Special Case -40°C = -40°F

This means that at -40 degrees, you do not need to perform a conversion to know the numerical value in the other scale.

When Is Celsius to Fahrenheit Conversion Useful?

Converting between Celsius and Fahrenheit can be useful in many situations.

  • Checking weather forecasts from another country.
  • Travelling internationally.
  • Following recipes that use a different temperature scale.
  • Understanding scientific information.
  • Comparing temperatures from different sources.
